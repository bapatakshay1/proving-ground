# Agents can pay today but seldom shop

Proving Ground can be made findable and payable by autonomous agents within a week for close to zero cost, but the evidence through October 5, 2026 says the "diner" will seat a trickle of developer-operated coding agents and a few crawlers, not a stream of budget-holding machine buyers. The channels that actually deliver agent traffic are the official MCP Registry (free, same-day listing, hourly fan-out to Glama, PulseMCP and GitHub) and Anthropic's Claude Connectors Directory (automatic Community listing for any paid plan); the clients that call new MCP servers are Claude Code, Codex and Cursor (64% of third-party calls at PostHog), while A2A agent cards, llms.txt and DNS records receive effectively zero requests. The one rail that lets a machine pay a stranger with no human involved is **x402 USDC on Base through Coinbase's CDP facilitator: 1,000 free settlements a month, then $0.001 each, no chargebacks**; Stripe cards cannot charge below $0.50 and net 67 cents on a dollar. But x402's real volume, once wash and test traffic is filtered, is roughly **$15M lifetime across ~5,300 sellers at a 14-cent average**, and nobody today sells per-verified-task grading to agents; what agents buy is data feeds, sanctions checks and browser sessions. The money in sealed environments and verification is lab contracts of **six to seven figures per quarter** (Halluminate, a nine-person finance-vertical twin, claims mid-eight-figure ARR from four of the top five labs) and enterprise outcome deals, both won through research teams and investor networks, never through a registry. The recommendation is to open the diner this week (server.json, Claude directory, OpenAPI with `x-payment-info` for the x402 Bazaar, a plain landing page), wire the existing HTTP 402 seat meter to x402 so an agent can refill a seat without a human, charge about **$0.05 per verified case with free reads**, sell prepaid seats of $5 to $25 for repeat agents, and treat diner revenue as a demo funnel worth hundreds to low thousands of dollars a month, not as a replacement for the original per-verified-outcome enterprise thesis or a lab environment contract.

Throughout, figures are tagged as **verified** (official docs, filings, or financial documents reported by a named outlet), **founder claim** (stated by a company about itself), **estimate** (analyst or anonymous-source), or **secondary** (reported only via an intermediary page). Conflicts between sources are called out where they occur.

## Registries and coding agents, not open-web search, route agent traffic

Agents do not wander onto new services in 2026. The traffic that reaches a third-party MCP server arrives through three doors: metadata registries that aggregators and IDE galleries poll, vendor-curated directories inside Claude and ChatGPT, and the developers who read a landing page and paste a URL into their coding agent. Open-web runtime discovery by agents is plausible but unmeasured, and the machine-readable manifests that were supposed to enable it go unread.

The **official MCP Registry** at registry.modelcontextprotocol.io is still "in preview" and is explicitly "intended to be consumed primarily by downstream aggregators, such as MCP server marketplaces," which are expected to poll it "once per hour"; it does no security scanning and charges no fee, and namespaces are claimed by GitHub login (`io.github.user/server`) or DNS/HTTP challenge ([modelcontextprotocol.io](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/about); [official requirements](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/registry/blob/main/docs/reference/server-json/official-registry-requirements.md)). A practitioner who published on September 20, 2026 confirmed the fan-out is real: the registry is "free, no review required," read "hourly" by Glama, PulseMCP, neuronto and reportedly GitHub's registry, with traffic to the endpoint confirmed, while DNS-AID `_agents` records, RFC 9727 API catalogs and agent-skills indexes produced nothing because "no resolver, crawler or client queries" them ([ryanlindsey.me issue #308](https://github.com/ryanlindsey/ryanlindsey.me/issues/308)). Another team's entry "appeared on the registry homepage's 'Recently Updated' section the same day," though they reported no traffic numbers and hit undocumented gotchas such as a 100-character description cap enforced only server-side ([dev.to, Sep 29, 2026](https://dev.to/edison_flores_6d2cd381b13/we-got-into-the-official-mcp-registry-the-parts-the-docs-dont-cover-9fh)). The registry's data quality is poor: SafeDep found roughly **64.7 million entries for about 1,691 unique packages** because republishing is allowed and CI loops duplicate entries, concluding the registry "solved discovery but not trust" ([SafeDep](https://safedep.io/the-state-of-mcp-registries/)). Position in a list that size is not itself a traffic source; the value is being upstream of the galleries that developers actually browse. GitHub's MCP Registry lists 357 servers with install buttons and is the only major directory that exposes install counts, where Microsoft's Markitdown shows **188K+ installations** and the leaderboard is dominated by first-party servers from large vendors ([github.com/mcp](https://github.com/mcp)).

The **Claude Connectors Directory** became a self-serve channel on September 25, 2026, when Anthropic opened a submission portal "to developers on paid Claude plans"; approved listings appear "across Claude, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork," and publishers get "usage analytics show installs by product surface and version" plus "listing views and search terms driving traffic" ([claude.com blog](https://claude.com/blog/build-plugins-for-claude)). The technical bar is a remote HTTPS server, OAuth 2.0 if it acts on a user's account or no auth for public data, a `title` and `readOnlyHint` or `destructiveHint` on every tool, a documentation URL, privacy policy, support contact, icon and a reviewer test account ([submission docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)). Review is light: Anthropic "scans it automatically for policy compliance and, by default, lists it in the directory as a Community connector," and escalation to the Verified label is also automatic with "no separate application" ([verification docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/verification)). The directory held "950+ servers" by July 2026, a figure that is secondary and unsourced ([bex.co, Oct 4, 2026](https://bex.co/blog/2026/10/04/mcp-sdk-400m-downloads-agent-tool-governance)). One conflict to note: a third-party guide says a Team or Enterprise org is required, whereas the official doc says any paid plan ([sunpeak.ai](https://sunpeak.ai/blogs/claude-connector-directory-submission/) versus [claude.com docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)).

The **ChatGPT Apps directory** is a heavier and, for Proving Ground, poorer fit. Submission requires individual or business identity verification, one MCP server per app, a domain token at `/.well-known/openai-apps-challenge`, five positive and three negative test cases, a video walkthrough and a test account, and "OpenAI scans your hosted MCP server daily" ([developers.openai.com](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/deploy/submission)). The directory is small (123 apps on July 16, 2026, of which 9 were hands-on verified, per one tracker; 65 per another) and apps "may link out to external websites to complete purchases for physical goods only," with an OpenAI forum reply stating you cannot submit an app that monetizes digital products ([chatgptappsrank.com](https://chatgptappsrank.com/state-of-chatgpt-apps-2026); [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/chatgpt-app-monetization-apps-sdk/1372343/4)). A paid verification API has no path to revenue there today.

The free long-tail directories mostly fill themselves. PulseMCP listed **22,311 servers on July 16, 2026** and auto-ingests from the official registry and GitHub ([pulsemcp.com](https://www.pulsemcp.com/servers)). Glama auto-indexes open-source servers, runs license, security and health checks, and lets owners claim listings via a `glama.json` in the repo or at `/.well-known/glama.json`, which unlocks "analytics and usage telemetry" ([glama.ai FAQ](https://glama.ai/mcp/faq)); its introspection crawl of `tools/list` is reported as "the near-certain first arrival" at a newly listed endpoint ([ryanlindsey.me #308](https://github.com/ryanlindsey/ryanlindsey.me/issues/308)). Glama's size is reported as roughly 11,000 by one guide and "nearly 37,000" by another, a conflict that reflects dates and counting methods rather than growth ([securecoders](https://www.securecoders.com/blog/mcp-server-registry-guide); [roxyapi](https://roxyapi.com/blogs/mcp-registries-where-to-list-your-server)). Smithery requires manual submission of a public Streamable-HTTP URL, scans tools itself and falls back to a static card at `/.well-known/mcp/server-card.json` ([smithery.ai docs](https://smithery.ai/docs/build/publish)). The `awesome-remote-mcp-servers` list now gates on a Glama claim ([mongodb-js issue #1554](https://github.com/mongodb-js/mongodb-mcp-server/issues/1554)). None of these directories bills agents or pays creators; monetization lives only on small marketplaces that take 15 to 20% (MCP Marketplace, Apify) or in self-hosted 402 layers, and the per-call prices on those marketplaces "range from $0.03 to $0.25 per call" ([dev.to monetization survey](https://dev.to/kirothebot/the-state-of-mcp-monetization-in-2026-where-builders-actually-get-paid-34k9), author-reported figures; [dev.to developer guide](https://dev.to/shekharp1536/how-to-monetize-mcp-servers-in-2026-the-complete-developers-guide-6bk)).

### The manifests nobody fetches

The machine-readable discovery conventions are cheap to publish and nearly worthless as inbound channels. Ahrefs' May 2026 logs across 137,210 domains found that of roughly 38,000 valid `llms.txt` files, **97% received zero requests from anyone**, and of the ~1,100 that did, 96% of requests were bots, mostly non-AI, with ChatGPT and Perplexity retrieval bots at about 1% ([Search Engine Journal on Ahrefs](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/97-of-llms-txt-files-got-no-requests-ahrefs-data-shows/579478/)); SE Ranking found that removing llms.txt from its AI-citation model improved accuracy ([llmtxt.info](https://llmtxt.info/blog/llms-txt-adoption-is-not-usage/)). Google's A2A agent cards fare no better on the publishing side: a September 21, 2026 sweep found **421 cards across 27,840 catalogued API providers (1.5%)**, 209 of them appearing in September alone, with incumbents under 1% adoption and "83 percent of these providers also run an MCP server" ([API Evangelist](https://apievangelist.com/2026/09/21/a2a-adoption-is-421-of-27840-and-most-of-them-arrived-with-the-card/)); the July sweep found only 10 of 65 published cards fully conformant ([API Evangelist, Jul 29](https://apievangelist.com/2026/07/29/most-published-agent-cards-are-not-actually-a2a/)). MCP Server Cards remain a draft (SEP-2127) whose charter still lists "real-world clients with reach implementing the proposal" as an unmet success criterion, and the path is unsettled: one writeup gives `/.well-known/mcp-server-card`, Smithery and SEP-1649 give `/.well-known/mcp/server-card.json` ([Server Card charter](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/community/server-card/charter); [bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/07/08/mcp-server-cards-well-known-discovery); [smithery docs](https://smithery.ai/docs/build/publish)). A secondary claim that Claude Desktop and Cursor shipped Server Card support in April 2026 could not be verified against any primary source. Serve both card paths and an llms.txt because the cost is minutes, and expect nothing from them.

### What an agent does at runtime, and who is actually calling

Runtime discovery is intra-catalog. Anthropic's Tool Search loads deferred tool definitions on demand via regex or BM25 search, cutting token overhead about 85%, and the same mechanism now runs over MCP tools in Claude Code ([Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use)); the motivation is real, since one GitHub MCP server consumed "55,000 tokens before your agent reads a single word" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/rudratosh/your-github-mcp-server-costs-55000-tokens-before-your-agent-reads-a-single-word-4eah)). PostHog's study of **63 million tool calls over 90 days to September 16, 2026** found "agents overwhelmingly pick the first door," with a generic SQL tool drawing about 4x the traffic of specialized ones, and schema-reading at about 11% of calls ([posthog.com](https://posthog.com/blog/how-ai-agents-behave)). When an agent is asked to find a service it calls a search tool such as Exa or Tavily and reads ordinary HTML ([Braintrust comparison](https://www.braintrust.dev/articles/best-web-search-apis-mcps-ai-agents-2026)), so a landing page whose title, first paragraph and visible tool list carry the literal keywords a buyer would type outranks any manifest. Tool naming matters for the same reason inside both ChatGPT's directory, where the app name carries most search weight ([phiture](https://phiture.com/asostack/chat-gpt-app-directory/)), and BM25 tool search.

The installed base that will call a new server is coding agents operated by developers. PostHog's third-party calls split **Claude Code 32%, Codex 22%, Cursor 10%, Cowork 7%**, with Claude Desktop, Claude.ai, ChatGPT and the Agent SDK at 2 to 3% each and chat-based agents only about 7% of calls overall ([posthog.com](https://posthog.com/blog/how-ai-agents-behave)). That is the crucial correction to the diner metaphor: the customers are humans with agents, and the agent is the fork, not the diner. The ecosystem is large (400M+ monthly MCP SDK downloads, 17,000+ public servers, secondary aggregation at [bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/10/04/mcp-sdk-400m-downloads-agent-tool-governance)), but no public case study attributes customer acquisition for an API business to any agent directory; the PostHog and Open Targets posts are first-party tooling for existing users ([Open Targets](https://blog.opentargets.org/case-study-mcp-server/)), and no directory other than GitHub's publishes per-listing installs.

| Channel (publish order) | Gate and cost | What arrives, and when | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote Streamable-HTTP MCP server with OAuth, titled and annotated tools, npm package with `mcpName` | Engineering only | Nothing on its own; prerequisite for everything below | Verified requirements |
| `server.json` to official MCP Registry | Free; GitHub or DNS namespace | Crawler introspection (Glama, neuronto) within hours; hourly fan-out to PulseMCP, GitHub, Glama | Verified mechanics; no per-listing traffic data |
| Public repo + `glama.json` claim + PR to `awesome-remote-mcp-servers` | Free | Analytics unlocked; listing on the list "scraped by everything else" | Practitioner report |
| Claude Connectors Directory | Any paid plan; privacy policy, docs, test account | Automatic Community listing; install and search-term analytics; only one-click channel for non-developers | Verified; Community vs Verified visibility unknown |
| Landing page + OpenAPI (with `x-payment-info`) | Hours | Whatever web search produces; required for non-MCP frameworks and the x402 Bazaar | Inference from agent search behavior |
| Smithery, mcp.so manual submissions | Free | Small additional developer browsing | Secondary counts |
| Server cards (both paths), A2A card, llms.txt | Minutes | Near zero; read by Smithery, neuronto, registries only | Verified near-zero fetch rates |
| ChatGPT Apps | Identity verification, review artifacts, daily scan | Consumer reach, but no digital-goods monetization | Verified; skip for now |

A realistic expectation, which no source measures and is therefore an estimate, is that the first month after publishing brings a handful of crawlers within a day and tens of distinct human-operated coding agents, not thousands; the only channel where the magnitude could surprise upward is Claude's directory if automatic Verified escalation triggers, and Anthropic discloses nothing about how Community listings rank.

## x402 settles a nickel for a tenth of a cent; cards cannot

Only one rail meets the brief's "no human on the buyer side" test today. x402, launched by Coinbase in May 2025 and moved to V2 on December 11, 2025, returns an HTTP 402 with a price, the client signs a USDC authorization and retries, and a facilitator verifies and settles on chain; V2 added CAIP chain identifiers, `PAYMENT-REQUIRED`/`PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`/`PAYMENT-RESPONSE` headers, wallet-based sessions and a discovery extension ([x402.org V2 post](https://www.x402.org/writing/x402-v2-launch); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/01/x402-agentic-http-payments/)). Governance moved to an **x402 Foundation under the Linux Foundation on April 2, 2026**, which reached 40 members by July 14, 2026, with Premier members including Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, AWS, Google, Cloudflare, Circle, Shopify and American Express, and USDC on Base as the primary settlement asset ([TFTC](https://www.tftc.io/x402-foundation-operational-launch-ai-agent-payments); [member analysis](https://agentpaytrend.com/x402-foundation-members-5-strategic-blocs/)).

The seller economics are the best available on any rail. Coinbase's CDP facilitator charges nothing for the **first 1,000 settled payments per month and $0.001 per settlement after that**, verification is always free, the facilitator pays gas so neither side needs native tokens, and OFAC and KYT screening are built in; it supports Base, Base Sepolia, Polygon, Arbitrum, World and Solana ([CDP facilitator docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/core-concepts/facilitator); fee start date January 1, 2026 per [CoinbaseDev](https://x.com/CoinbaseDev/status/1995564027951665551)). Seller setup is a CDP API key, an `X402_PAY_TO` address, and Express or FastAPI middleware that prices routes with strings like `"$0.01"` on network `eip155:8453` ([CDP seller quickstart](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/quickstart-for-sellers)); an official guide covers MCP servers specifically ([docs.x402.org](https://docs.x402.org/guides/mcp-server-with-x402)). Older guides describing the facilitator as fee-free are superseded by the tiered schedule. PayAI runs an independent facilitator at "network gas costs plus 30%," about 1.5 to 2.3 credits of $0.001 per settlement with 1,000 free credits per receiving wallet, and needs no merchant credentials for ordinary `exact` payments ([payai.network](https://payai.network/)); one seller who self-hosted a facilitator after CDP's billing KYC rejected their documents three times reports **about $0.0005 per settlement plus a $10 ETH reserve** at 869 payments a day ([dev.to](https://dev.to/whiteknightonhorse/how-we-replaced-our-x402-payment-facilitator-in-a-single-afternoon-f84)). At a $0.05 call the CDP fee is 2% at scale and zero under 1,000 calls a month; there is no percentage fee and no chargeback because the exact scheme is "irreversible once executed," with refunds being "a new USDC transfer back" ([x402 FAQ](https://x402.gitbook.io/x402/faq)).

Stripe offers the fiat-friendly alternative, at a price. The Machine Payments Protocol (MPP), launched March 18, 2026 with the Tempo chain and design partners including Anthropic, OpenAI, Visa and Mastercard, uses the same 402 challenge shape ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/394131/tempo-mainnet-goes-live-with-machine-payments-protocol-for-agents)); Stripe's docs show `npm install mppx stripe`, a preview API version (`2026-07-29.preview`), stablecoin payments on Tempo, Solana and x402-on-Base that "land directly in your Stripe balance and settle in fiat," a **0.01 USDC minimum for stablecoins and $0.50 for card Shared Payment Tokens**, availability in all US states except New York for stablecoins, and a Stripe Directory that sellers join by emailing their llms.txt ([Stripe MPP docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine/mpp); [Stripe machine payments overview](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)). Stripe can also record x402 settlements as PaymentIntents in `transaction_verification` mode, which gives fiat accounting on top of CDP ([Stripe x402 docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine/x402)). The stablecoin fee is a conflict: secondary coverage reports **1.5% flat** ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15083174-stripe-stablecoin-payments-2026); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/stripe-charges-1-5-stablecoin-145737023.html)), while Stripe's own pricing page lists "Link ... Stablecoins: 0.8% per transaction," and the non-Link machine-payments rate was not confirmed on the fetched page ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)). Either figure is fine at five cents. Cards are not: Stripe's minimum charge is $0.50, domestic cards cost 2.9% + $0.30 so a $1 charge nets $0.67, disputes cost $15, and Shared Payment Tokens, the only card design where an agent pays a seller it has never met, add **$0.15 per token issued** ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing); [Stripe currencies](https://docs.stripe.com/currencies); [SPT docs](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/concepts/shared-payment-tokens)). Cards therefore belong only on prepaid packs of $5 or more. ChatGPT's Instant Checkout was retired in March 2026 by multiple accounts, but that was consumer retail; SPTs and the ACP spec survive inside MPP ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/openai-abandons-instant-checkout-feature-in-chatgpt/)).

The card-network frameworks are not rails a solo API seller plugs into. Google donated AP2 to the FIDO Alliance on April 28, 2026 with a v0.2 "human not present" mode, but it is a mandate-signing standard with nothing live for merchants in the announcement ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/google-pay/agent-payments-protocol-fido-alliance/)); Visa Intelligent Commerce Connect entered pilot April 8, 2026 via the Visa Acceptance Platform ([Visa](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22276.html)); Mastercard's "Agent Pay for Machines," the first network product aimed at M2M microtransactions, was announced June 10, 2026 with 30+ partners, no fees, and no live transactions ([Mastercard](https://investor.mastercard.com/investor-news/investor-news-details/2026/Mastercard-Launches-Agent-Pay-for-Machines-to-Unlock-Super-Fast-Always-On-Payments/default.aspx)); PayPal's Agentic Commerce Services target merchant catalogs in shopping assistants ([PayPal](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2025-10-28-PayPal-Launches-Agentic-Commerce-Services-to-Power-AI-Driven-Shopping)). All ride on a human cardholder's credential and reach sellers through a PSP. Among the alternatives, Nevermined meters credits over x402 and Stripe at 1 to 2% of volume ([Eco fee comparison](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14895614-stablecoin-payment-processor-fees-compared-2026)), L402 is live Lightning tooling that settles in BTC and requires a node ([Lightning Labs](https://lightning.engineering/posts/2026-03-11-L402-for-agents/)), Skyfire has no published fee schedule, and Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway (September 30, 2026) wraps CDP's facilitator for US sellers in a closed beta with undisclosed fees and a promised but not shipped seller-discovery feature ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway-beta/)); Cloudflare's pay-per-crawl became "pay per use" for publisher content on July 1, 2026 with no disclosed pricing and is not an API billing product ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/cloudflare-stops-charging-ai-per-crawl-and-starts-paying-per-answer/)).

### Who can actually pay, and how much is real

The buyer-side runtimes that can answer a 402 are developer-built. Coinbase AgentKit binds x402 payment into LangChain, the Vercel AI SDK, the OpenAI Agents SDK and MCP ([Crossmint comparison](https://www.crossmint.com/learn/agent-wallets-compared)), and Coinbase's Agentic Wallets, launched February 11, 2026, add session caps, per-transaction caps and risk screening ([Coinbase CDP](https://www.coinbase.com/developer-platform/discover/launches/agentic-wallets)). Community MCP servers give Claude, Cursor and similar clients a paying wallet, and the x402 client applies a **default $1 spend cap** unless overridden ([x402node/x402-mcp](https://github.com/x402node/x402-mcp)). Stripe ships `@stripe/link-cli` for SPTs, `purl` for x402 and a Tempo CLI for MPP ([Stripe MPP docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine/mpp)), and Cloudflare's Agents SDK can pay x402 tools ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/x402/)). No source shows Claude.ai, ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot paying a 402 natively. Coinbase counted **69,000 active agents** in late April 2026, a secondary figure ([Nevermined roundup](https://nevermined.ai/blog/stablecoin-payments-ai-agents-statistics)). Prices in the cents-to-low-dollars range match these guardrails; a $5 call may be blocked by default caps.

How much of x402 is real commerce is the most conflicted question in this research. Coinbase's Q2 2026 reporting, reachable only through secondary coverage, claims **205M cumulative transactions and about $53M cumulative volume**, with Coinbase at 67% of activity and 97%+ of onchain agentic transactions using x402 ([Cryptorank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/c619a-ai-agents-205-million-transactions-x402-coinbase)). The public counter on x402.org has shown **75.41M transactions and $24.24M "frozen since March 2026,"** while an Artemis/Visa filtered analysis as of April 21, 2026 counted 109.6M transactions and about **$15.0M of real volume from $135.7M raw, with wash, test and internal transfers at 89% of raw dollar volume, roughly 5,300 lifetime sellers, a 14-cent average payment, and about $28,400 a day** versus Q4 2025 peaks near $800,000 ([McGlynn, Sep 6, 2026](https://www.danielmcglynn.com/the-x402-counter-has-shown-the-same-four-numbers-since-march/)). CoinDesk's March 11, 2026 analysis found about $28,000 a day with "roughly 50%" gamed and quoted an Artemis analyst calling the boom "mostly a mirage" ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/11/coinbase-backed-ai-payments-protocol-wants-to-fix-micropayment-but-demand-is-just-not-there-yet)); its July 15 piece reported 75M transactions moving $24M in 30 days while noting DefiLlama tracked only $572k over the same window, a two-order-of-magnitude gap the article itself flags ([CoinDesk, Jul 15](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/07/15/visa-mastercard-and-ripple-join-the-standard-letting-ai-agents-pay-in-stablecoins)). Chainalysis documented a memecoin (PING) generating 150k+ transactions in a month, retention peaking at 87% during that rush and falling to 5%, and the mix shifting so that **$1+ transactions became 95% of volume** ([Chainalysis, Jun 3, 2026](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption/)). A "$600M annualized" figure from BlockEden is an extrapolation that contradicts every cumulative count ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/02/20/x402-protocol-enterprise-ai-agent-payments/)). Circle's own developer post is candid: "adding a 402 to your API won't bring in money by itself. Demand is still catching up" ([Circle](https://www.circle.com/blog/turn-your-api-into-a-storefront-for-agents)). The honest reading is that the rail works, costs almost nothing, and addresses a market whose real daily turnover across all sellers is in the tens of thousands of dollars.

## Labs pay six figures per quarter; agents pay fourteen cents

The environment, evaluation and verification market splits into a large lab-facing tier with relationship-driven sales, a nascent enterprise tier with no price discovery, developer eval platforms metered per trace, and an agent-callable tier that exists only on x402 and sells data rather than evaluation. Proving Ground sits, by product shape, in the first tier and is trying to sell in the fourth.

The lab tier is real and concentrated. rl-list counted **38 vendors on July 15, 2026**, with 12 funded under $10M, four incumbents at $200M+, and "pricing and detailed customer lists are not publicly disclosed" ([rl-list](https://www.rl-list.com/)); Deedy Das estimated more than 50 companies selling data and RL environments at about $8.5B combined revenue with over 75% in Scale, Surge, Mercor and Handshake, a post that could not be fetched directly (estimate, [X](https://x.com/deedydas/status/2076124392711696455)). Anthropic's spend is the headline conflict: The Information reported in September 2025 that leaders "discussed spending over $1 billion on RL environments over the next year" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/21/silicon-valley-bets-big-on-environments-to-train-ai-agents/)), Wing VC estimated in January 2026 that the actual spend was "tens of millions annually" growing 3 to 5x ([Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/rl-environments-for-agentic-ai-who-will-win-the-training-verification-layer-by-2030)), and Nathan Lambert relayed hearsay of "$10M+ for single environments" and "hundreds of millions per year" ([Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/notes-from-inside-chinas-ai-labs)). The honest bracket is tens to low hundreds of millions, with $1B an aspirational ceiling. Unit prices come from anonymous founders and researchers: contracts of "seven figures per quarter or more" according to a founder versus "$300k–$500k" per quarter according to a neolab researcher, **exclusivity at 4 to 5x**, about $20k per website UI gym, about $300k for a Slack-class clone, $200 to $2,000 per expert task, and about $2,400 of compute per task during training ([Epoch AI, Jan 12, 2026](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/state-of-rl-envs); [Sapphire Ventures, Apr 13, 2026](https://sapphireventures.com/blog/reinforcement-learning-environments-ai-agents/)). The only verified revenue in the sector is Mercor's **$614M in H1 2026, 91% from foundation-model companies**, from financial documents reported by The Information ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/865c23cb-3e0d-4c21-aa0d-6bf2a7fc8d1a)). Everything else is a founder claim or estimate: Fleet's $60M run-rate is a Sacra estimate, with early revenue from "bespoke environment builds for large financial services and insurance firms" before labs became the main buyer ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/fleet/)); AfterQuery disclosed a >$100M run-rate and a $3.2B valuation ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/cf799168-a31d-48f7-9ee1-b159af751eaa)); Mechanize raised $9.1M at $500M and was in talks with Google in August 2026 for a >$1.5B hire-plus-license deal whose closing could not be verified ([Mechanize](https://www.mechanize.work/mechanize-raises-9-1m/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/05/google-targets-ai-startup-mechanizes-technology-talent-proposed-1-5b-deal/)).

The direct comparable for Proving Ground is **Halluminate**, a finance-vertical "data research lab" selling managed sandboxes, benchmarks and expert evals, which announced a $30M Series A led by Oak HC/FT on October 2, 2026 with nine employees; its CEO says "four of the top five closed-source U.S. AI labs" pay for it and that it is "profitable at a mid-eight-figure annualized revenue run rate," which is a founder claim with no named customers, and its coverage frames the next milestone as landing a bank, brokerage or insurer willing to be named, meaning no enterprise reference exists yet ([AI Weekly on Fortune](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/halluminate-raises-30m-to-build-finance-ai-training-labs); [ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/halluminate-30m-series-a-ai-training-environments-2026)). Scale says "nearly half of all of our new data training projects involve reinforcement learning environments" ([Scale AI](https://scale.com/blog/rl-environments)). Prime Intellect crowdsources environments with bounties of $100 to $500 open and $1,000 to $5,000+ by application, and monetizes compute rather than environments ([Prime Intellect](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/scaling-environments-program); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/08/prime-intellect-raises-130m-1b-valuation-ai-training-platform/)). HUD rents hosted environments at **$0.10 per environment-hour** on its home page but "$0.25+" on an April 2026 resource page, a discrepancy that likely reflects a tier or price change, and runs a vendor marketplace with no published revenue share ([hud.ai](https://www.hud.ai/); [HUD resources](https://www.hud.ai/resources/best-platforms-publishing-rl-environments-model-labs)).

How these customers are found is unambiguous and unflattering to registries. Lab research teams "identify capability gaps and write task specs that outline which domains it wants to cover, how hard the tasks should be, and what the verifier needs to check for," and vendors build to that spec ([Sapphire](https://sapphireventures.com/blog/reinforcement-learning-environments-ai-agents/)); labs choose on "research impact rather than procurement formalities" ([Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/rl-environments-for-agentic-ai-who-will-win-the-training-verification-layer-by-2030)); Anthropic works with 12+ vendors and is "building a vendor engagement platform to commoditize environments," while OpenAI builds an in-house data team ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/rl-environments-and-rl-for-science)). The pure-play winners share frontier-adjacent angels (Nat Friedman, Daniel Gross, AI Grant, Sequoia). rl-list itself says vendors "recommend direct engagement for work samples and cost evaluation," and HUD's claim that its marketplace needs "no separate outreach" is the vendor's own marketing ([rl-list](https://www.rl-list.com/); [HUD](https://www.hud.ai/resources/best-platforms-publishing-rl-environments-model-labs)). No source attributes a frontier-lab contract to any public registry.

The enterprise twin tier is pre-price-discovery. Veris AI sells "as-service ... with a usage-based pricing model" but publishes no prices and lists cost drivers as tools mocked, runs per week, concurrency and deployment mode ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2025/06/veris-ai-enterprise-ai-agents-agentic-simulation-based-training-platform-mehdi-jamei/); [Veris pricing page](https://llms.veris.ai/veris-ai-pricing-roi)); Sapphire describes enterprise RL enablers as "services-heavy, often embedding engineers with customers," and Wing says enterprises adopt via "forward-deployed, outcome-oriented solutions" ([Sapphire](https://sapphireventures.com/blog/reinforcement-learning-environments-ai-agents/); [Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/rl-environments-for-agentic-ai-who-will-win-the-training-verification-layer-by-2030)). That is the shape of the original Proving Ground thesis, and the market evidence says it is bought as a services-led outcome contract, not a metered API.

Developer eval platforms show what verification is worth when sold per unit to humans: Braintrust meters scores at **$2.50 per 1,000** ($1.50 overage on the $249/month Pro plan), LangSmith charges $0.50 per 1,000 base traces, Langfuse runs $29 to $199 a month, Arize $50 ([benchmarkingagents.com](https://benchmarkingagents.com/tools-compared/)); none can be bought by an agent without a human-created account. The judge cost that dominates LLM-graded evals (a single PaperBench run at about $9,500 including the judge, per [ContextQA](https://contextqa.com/blog/real-cost-of-ai-agent-evaluation/)) is precisely the cost Proving Ground avoids with a code verifier, which is its structural pricing advantage. The agent-callable tier exists only on x402: the Bazaar lists "100+ services" priced "from $0.005 per call" ([x402 Bazaar](https://www.x402bazaar.org/)), Browserbase sells browser sessions over x402 with no disclosed usage ([Browserbase](https://www.browserbase.com/blog/browserbase-and-coinbase-x402)), and Chainalysis reports what agents actually buy is "premium data feeds, sanctions screening, and credit bureau queries" ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption/)). No vendor sells per-verified-task grading to third-party agents; the research found none, which makes Proving Ground's diner both unprecedented and unproven.

### Agent marketplaces: enormous counts, tiny dollars

The agent-as-customer marketplaces confirm the pattern. Olas, in a post titled "Approaching 20 Million Olas Agent Transactions: Spam or...," reports 19.9M on-chain transactions but **marketplace lifetime turnover of $107,808.60 and lifetime protocol fees of $546.46** ([Olas, Aug 14, 2026](https://olas.network/blog/20-million-olas-agent-transactions)). Virtuals advertises "$479M Agentic GDP" and 1.77M completed jobs beside "cumulative agent revenue $1.16 million+" and a Revenue Network paying "up to $1 million per month" to agents that sell through it, so the subsidy roughly equals the revenue ([bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/04/04/virtuals-protocol-ai-economic-operating-system-on-chain-agent-economy); [agenteconomy.to](https://agenteconomy.to/virtuals-acp)). The verdict on "real market versus hype" is therefore split by product: agents paying cents for data, compliance and API primitives is real and small; agents autonomously buying environments or verification of themselves does not yet exist at any observable scale.

The "sold once" dynamic that haunts the lab tier also matters for pricing the diner. Ben Anderson's argument is that once models master a skill, environment value tends "to 0, as models can now provide unlimited, cheap data as a substitute," so "the only actually-sustainable business here is the ops business" ([benanderson.work](https://benanderson.work/blog/dont-build-rl-env-startup/)); Mercor's acquisitions of Sepal AI and Deeptune in 2026 and Google's Mechanize talks show labs and incumbents internalizing capability. The automation floor is dropping fast: ClawEnvKit generated **1,040 environments for about $80** of Sonnet 4.6 calls, roughly $0.08 each and "13,800x" cheaper than hand curation ([arXiv 2604.18543](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18543v2)). Recurring revenue in this market comes from ops incumbents, metered runtime (HUD, Prime Intellect), or vertical specialists that keep generating harder tasks (Halluminate's benchmark-then-environment loop). A finance-ops twin with a verifier and proof packets is a Halluminate-shaped asset; the diner is a way to keep it generating fresh, verified data.

## A US solo founder can be legally paid by an agent in one week

None of the setup is exotic, and the legal position of a merchant accepting USDC for its own service is clearer than the agent-commerce hype suggests. These are planning notes, not legal or tax advice, and no primary authority squarely addresses micro-payments from autonomous agents' wallets; the analysis rests on the general merchant exemption.

On federal money-transmission law, FinCEN's 2019 guidance exempts a person who "accepts and transmits funds only integral to the sale of goods or the provision of services," classifies a party who obtains virtual currency to buy goods or services as a "user," and states that "exchangers and administrators generally qualify as money transmitters under the BSA, while users do not"; the regulated party in the chain is the CVC payment processor, which "fall[s] within the definition of a money transmitter" ([FinCEN FIN-2019-G001](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf)). Two cautions follow from the same document: FinCEN "interprets these exemptions strictly," and MSB status does not depend on whether the business "operates ... through mechanical or software agents." The exemption holds as long as Proving Ground never holds or forwards funds for others, so credits must not become transferable stored value and the service must not broker payments between agents and third parties. State licensing is "generally" outside the definition for merchants but is "a state-by-state legal determination, not a rule of thumb" ([Cornerstone Licensing](https://cornerstonelicensing.com/who-needs-a-money-transmitter-license)). Using CDP as facilitator pushes settlement, KYT screening and gas onto Coinbase; sellers "don't need any Coinbase products" and "never hold the buyer's key" ([x402 FAQ](https://x402.gitbook.io/x402/faq)), but one seller reports CDP demanded KYC documents to enable billing past the free tier, rejected them three times with "no support channel for KYC issues," and settlements would have failed when the 1,000 free monthly payments ran out ([dev.to](https://dev.to/whiteknightonhorse/how-we-replaced-our-x402-payment-facilitator-in-a-single-afternoon-f84)). A PayAI or self-hosted fallback is therefore part of the minimum setup, not an optimization.

Tax treatment is mechanical. The IRS page updated September 2, 2026 states that a digital asset received "in exchange for goods or services in a business context" is ordinary income at US-dollar fair market value on receipt, reported on Schedule C, with later disposition a capital event with basis equal to that value, and the digital-asset question on Form 1040 must be answered "Yes" ([IRS digital assets](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets)). At $1.00 per USDC the gain on conversion is near zero but each sweep to Coinbase is still a logged disposition. Broker reporting on Form 1099-DA is the processor's duty and may cover "certain processors of digital asset payments" with de minimis thresholds for stablecoins ([IRS broker regulations](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/final-regulations-and-related-irs-guidance-for-reporting-by-brokers-on-sales-and-exchanges-of-digital-assets)); Form 8300 digital-asset reporting is suspended until final regulations ([TaxBit](https://www.taxbit.com/blogs/digital-asset-forms-8300-are-delayed-along-with-crypto-broker-reporting)); Stripe's 1099-K federal threshold is **more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions** for 2025 and 2026 after OBBBA, with some states at $600 ([1099online](https://www.1099online.com/blog/form-1099-k-threshold/)). Sales tax nexus is typically $100,000 or 200 transactions, $500,000 in California and Texas, with SaaS taxability varying by state ([Stripe nexus guide](https://stripe.com/guides/introduction-to-us-sales-tax-and-economic-nexus); [Numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/economic-nexus)); at diner volumes it is a monitoring item, not a week-one task.

Accounts are the gating items. A US Stripe account needs a physical US address, a representative physically in the US for unregistered businesses, and verification of legal name, EIN, SSN or ITIN; sole proprietors can use an SSN and a linked bank account and should expect a request for a live website URL ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/requirements-for-having-a-us-stripe-account); [Wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/stripe-sole-proprietor)). Coinbase Business is "available to US-registered business entities," which argues for an LLC, and a secondary source says the off-ramp application "can take up to two weeks"; retail Coinbase accounts sell USDC 1:1 with zero spread and withdraw by ACH free, wire $25, or instant card payout at 1.5% ([Coinbase help](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/other-topics/business/us-entity-non-us-beneficial-owner-eligibility); [Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15039728-convert-usdc-to-bank-account-fastest-routes-in-2026)). Whether a sole proprietor without an LLC can open Coinbase Business could not be verified because Coinbase pages returned 403. The documented custody pattern is an operator wallet holding about $10 of ETH for gas, separate from a receiver wallet in cold custody that is the x402 `payTo` address and never lives on the server ([dev.to](https://dev.to/whiteknightonhorse/how-we-replaced-our-x402-payment-facilitator-in-a-single-afternoon-f84)).

Terms of service for a machine customer are enforceable and have templates. California's UETA section 14 provides that "a contract may be formed by the interaction of electronic agents of the parties, even if no individual was aware of or reviewed the electronic agents' actions," and UETA is enacted in 49 states plus DC with New York's ESRA as the analogue ([Cal. Civ. Code 1633.14](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1633.14.&lawCode=CIV); [NYC Bar](https://www.nycbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/20221468-UETA_ESRA.pdf)). Proskauer flags the open questions, including attribution when an agent "exceed[s] budget parameters" and that "absent express protective provisions, users likely bear legal risk," and no case law yet applies section 14 to LLM agents ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/contract-law-in-the-age-of-agentic-ai-whos-really-clicking-accept)). The vendor precedents converge: Tavily's May 4, 2026 terms bind the customer for use "through an Agent Key by Customer, its agents, or any person or system acting on Customer's behalf," make all fees non-refundable, cap liability at three months of fees, and reserve the right to change usage caps and rate limits ([Tavily terms](https://www.tavily.com/terms)); Exa caps liability at the greater of $100 or six months of fees ([Exa terms](https://exa.ai/terms)); Firecrawl gives no refunds, caps at six months, and publishes its charge-on-error rule ("a scrape that returns no result is not charged," a 403 or 404 "costs 1 credit") ([Firecrawl terms](https://www.firecrawl.dev/terms-of-service); [Firecrawl pricing](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing)). Because x402 payments are irreversible and Stripe stablecoin payments carry "Dispute support: No" ([Stripe crypto docs](https://docs.stripe.com/crypto/pay-with-crypto)), chargeback exposure comes only from card-funded packs; the ToS URL belongs in the 402 response body and the OpenAPI document, since whether a 402 body gives adequate notice has no direct authority.

Metering and abuse controls have a documented threat model. The "Five Attacks on x402" paper (May 12, 2026) catalogs revert-grant (resource released before finality), replay of one payment across requests "without server-side deduplication," cache leakage of paid responses, and discovery steering, and prescribes treating the payment header "as a single-use capability" by atomically claiming the (pay_id, resource_id) pair before serving, settling before serving for valuable resources, and `Cache-Control: no-store, private` on gated responses ([arXiv 2605.11781](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11781v1)). Clients double-pay on disconnect-retry because the two payments carry different nonces ([automaton issue #393](https://github.com/Conway-Research/automaton/issues/393)), so honoring an `Idempotency-Key` and returning the cached verdict is the only fix. Free tiers are a measured liability: Stripe's fraud research, via a secondary report, shows a **6.2x increase in abusive free trials between November 2025 and February 2026**, with API-first AI startups seeing "roughly 10x more attempted abuse than enterprise AI sellers" and abuse consuming 10 to 25% of free-tier capacity ([Zylos](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-08-12-free-trial-abuse-registration-risk-agent-platforms/)); Stripe's own guidance is credential-level rate limits and progressive gating ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/how-to-prevent-free-trial-abuse-in-saas-and-ai-products)). Apify's agent portal shows the account-free alternative: pay by x402 or MPP, **minimum prepaid $1**, receive a bearer token with a 14-day TTL, unused credit non-refundable ([agi.apify.com](https://agi.apify.com/)). A paid first call at a cent is a better abuse filter than any signup form. The ledger should be an append-only event stream keyed by payment nonce or tx hash with payer, resource, status, facilitator verify and settle payloads, timestamps and USD amount, with "rate limiting as a service protection control and metering as a financial evidence pipeline" ([Ordway](https://ordwaylabs.com/blog/event-metering-for-usage-based-billing/)).

## Proving Ground should open the diner as a funnel, not a business

The recommendation follows from putting the three bodies of evidence together: discovery is cheap but delivers developers with agents rather than autonomous buyers; x402 makes a five-cent verified case economically settleable with no human; and the verified money in this category comes from labs and enterprises who buy through people. The diner should be built in week one because it costs almost nothing, filters abuse better than a free tier, and gives a lab researcher or enterprise evaluator a zero-friction way to point an agent at the twin. It should not be the revenue plan.

On channels, publish in the order the first section lays out and stop before ChatGPT Apps. Proving Ground already has the remote MCP endpoint, seats and a 402 meter, so the incremental work is a `server.json` under a DNS-verified `com.<domain>/proving-ground` namespace or `io.github.<user>/proving-ground`, a public repo with `glama.json`, a Claude directory submission with a reviewer seat, and a landing page whose title and first paragraph say "accounts-payable exception environment with pass/fail verifier and proof packet" in plain words, because that is the string a BM25 tool search or an Exa query will match. Tool design matters more than listing count: keep three or four obviously named tools (list cases, get case, submit resolution, get proof packet) with `title`, `readOnlyHint` and `destructiveHint`, since agents "pick the first door" and both directory search and tool search reward literal keyword matches ([posthog.com](https://posthog.com/blog/how-ai-agents-behave); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/advanced-tool-use)). List in the x402 Bazaar through an OpenAPI `x-payment-info` block, which one seller found the registry required instead of `/.well-known/x402` ([dev.to](https://dev.to/levelsofself/we-put-a-paid-api-into-the-agent-economy-in-one-morning-x402-self-hosted-dd)), and email Stripe to join its Directory once stablecoin acceptance is approved ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/machine)). Expect crawlers within a day and the first human-operated coding agents within weeks; expect nothing from cards or llms.txt.

On rails, adopt x402 via CDP on Base USDC as the primary headless path, with PayAI or a self-hosted facilitator configured from day one as the fallback for the KYC and free-tier cliff. Add Stripe for prepaid seats bought by humans with cards at $5 or more and, when the stablecoin method is approved, for agents paying by Stripe MPP; optionally mirror x402 settlements into Stripe as `transaction_verification` PaymentIntents for fiat books. Skip SPTs below $5 packs given the $0.15 token fee and $0.50 floor, skip L402 and the card networks entirely for now, and watch Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway for its promised seller-discovery feature since it wraps the same CDP facilitator ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/monetization-gateway-beta/)).

On price, the unit should be the verified claim, meaning one case worked to a verdict with its proof packet, and the recommended list price is **$0.05 per verified case**, with listing and reading cases free, and a "hard" or multi-step case tier at $0.10. Five reasons, each anchored in the evidence. First, the floor: at $0.01 the CDP fee is 10% of ticket after the free tier, which the x402 pricing literature treats as the minimum sensible ticket ([stablecoininsider](https://stablecoininsider.org/how-to-price-an-x402-api-in-usdc/)); $0.05 keeps the fee at 2%. Second, the ceiling: the default x402 client spend cap is $1 and Agentic Wallets enforce session caps, so anything above a dollar risks silent refusal ([x402node/x402-mcp](https://github.com/x402node/x402-mcp)). Third, comparables: commodity primitives clear at $0.001 to $0.01 per call (Serper $0.30 to $1.00 per 1,000, Browserbase fetch $1 per 1,000, Tavily $0.005 to $0.008 per credit) while differentiated agent work prices higher (Exa agent runs $0.012 to $1.00, Tavily research up to 250 credits) and marketplace MCP tools sit at $0.03 to $0.25 ([Serper](https://serper.dev/); [Browserbase pricing](https://www.browserbase.com/pricing); [Tavily pricing](https://www.tavily.com/pricing); [Exa pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing); [dev.to](https://dev.to/shekharp1536/how-to-monetize-mcp-servers-in-2026-the-complete-developers-guide-6bk)); a code-verified verdict with a proof packet is differentiated work, and the observed x402 average payment of 14 to 32 cents says buyers already pay in that band ([McGlynn](https://www.danielmcglynn.com/the-x402-counter-has-shown-the-same-four-numbers-since-march/); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/07/15/visa-mastercard-and-ripple-join-the-standard-letting-ai-agents-pay-in-stablecoins)). Fourth, cost structure: Braintrust's $0.0015 to $0.0025 per score shows the platform margin on verification is thin when a judge model is involved, but Proving Ground's verifier is code, so the marginal cost per case is compute and storage, and $0.05 is almost entirely margin. Fifth, signaling: the lab tier values a task at $200 to $2,000 as an asset ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/state-of-rl-envs)); charging cents per run on the diner does not cannibalize that, because labs buy the environment and verifier, not runs, and the proof-packet corpus the diner generates is itself the kind of benchmark artifact that supports a lab conversation. Publish the charge-on-error rule the way Firecrawl does: a verifier error or timeout is not charged, a submitted resolution that fails is charged, because the verdict is the product.

On settlement, run the hybrid that the Apify and x402 evidence supports and that Proving Ground's architecture already implies. The existing seat and credits meter is the prepaid layer; fund it by x402 at a **$1 minimum** or by card at $5, draw it down at the per-case rate, issue a short-TTL bearer key, and let the seat-empty 402 carry an x402 challenge for a refill so an agent never needs a human. Offer per-call `exact` settlement only for the anonymous first touch, which replaces the free tier with a cheap paid call that costs an attacker real money and gas. Prepaid wins on economics because it amortizes the $0.001 settlement fee across hundreds of cases, and it matches the seat abstraction labs and enterprises will expect to buy in bulk; `upto` and `batch-settlement` schemes are available later if per-call volume ever justifies them ([CDP facilitator docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/core-concepts/facilitator)).

### What the diner can honestly earn

The arithmetic is sobering. At $0.05 per case, 500 cases a month is $25, 10,000 is $500, and 100,000 is $5,000 gross with roughly 95% net on x402. The entire filtered x402 market turns over about **$28,400 a day, or about $850,000 a month, across roughly 5,300 lifetime sellers** ([McGlynn](https://www.danielmcglynn.com/the-x402-counter-has-shown-the-same-four-numbers-since-march/)), so a $5,000 month would make Proving Ground one of the larger sellers on the rail, and the two x402-native sellers who published their numbers report "Real money settled: not yet" and 869 payments a day at sub-cent tickets ([dev.to](https://dev.to/levelsofself/we-put-a-paid-api-into-the-agent-economy-in-one-morning-x402-self-hosted-dd); [dev.to](https://dev.to/whiteknightonhorse/how-we-replaced-our-x402-payment-facilitator-in-a-single-afternoon-f84)). PostHog's 63 million calls came to a free first-party server with 130,000 existing users, not a paid long-tail one. A single neolab environment contract at $300,000 to $500,000 a quarter is $100,000 to $167,000 a month ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/state-of-rl-envs)); the diner at its optimistic case is 3 to 5% of one small lab deal. The honest year-one range for diner revenue is tens to low thousands of dollars a month, with the upside gated on things outside the founder's control: Claude or ChatGPT paying 402s natively, Cloudflare shipping seller discovery, Mastercard's Agent Pay for Machines going live, or the x402 filtered volume trend reversing its decline from late-2025 peaks.

Relative to the original thesis of operating accounts-payable exception workflows for enterprises billed per verified outcome, the diner is a lower-ceiling, lower-friction sibling, not a substitute. The enterprise outcome model has no public price discovery, is bought as services-led, forward-deployed work, and has produced no named reference customer even for the best-funded finance-vertical vendor; it is slow and relationship-driven but it is where enterprise money is heading according to both VC analyses that looked ([Wing VC](https://www.wing.vc/content/rl-environments-for-agentic-ai-who-will-win-the-training-verification-layer-by-2030); [Sapphire](https://sapphireventures.com/blog/reinforcement-learning-environments-ai-agents/)). The lab model is faster and verified to pay six to seven figures a quarter, but is "sold once," priced 4 to 5x for exclusivity, and originates from research teams and angels rather than any channel a solo founder can publish to. The diner's strategic value is as the thing that makes both conversations easier: a lab researcher can run a thousand cases against Proving Ground with their own agent and a $50 seat before a call ever happens, the proof packets accumulate into a benchmark that is the artifact labs actually buy, and the agent-native payment path is a credible differentiator in outreach. Measured as a business on its own, it is a demo that pays for its own hosting.

## Conclusion

The diner metaphor inverts under the evidence. Agents in 2026 do not walk in off the street; developers seat them, and the directories that matter are the galleries those developers browse inside Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. What x402 changes is not who finds the diner but whether the fork can pay, and it can, for a tenth of a cent, with no chargeback and no human, which makes the cheap paid first call a better front door than any free tier and makes Proving Ground's existing 402 meter the right primitive to extend rather than replace. The market that would make the diner a business, agents autonomously buying verification of their own work, has no observable instance anywhere, and the adjacent market that exists, agents buying data and compliance checks, clears in cents across a few thousand sellers sharing tens of thousands of dollars a day.

The implication for Proving Ground is to treat the diner as instrumentation for the real sale. Every verified case run by a stranger's agent is a datapoint about where agents fail on accounts-payable exceptions, and that corpus, not the nickels, is what a Halluminate-shaped vendor sells to four of the top five labs and what a bank will eventually pay for per verified outcome. Build the diner this week, price it at a nickel, keep the proof packets, and spend the founder's scarce hours on the research teams and enterprise operators who sign the contracts the evidence says exist. Re-price the diner only if one of the gating events flips: a frontier assistant paying 402s natively, a platform shipping agent-side discovery of priced endpoints, or the filtered x402 volume curve turning up.
